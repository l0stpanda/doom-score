**Final choice (D76, D81).** **MLB: B1** (OLS on `win_pct_percentile`). B1 is the best MLB model outright; the elastic net, horizon EN and EBM are within noise of it but not better (elastic net Δ Spearman −0.010 [−0.064, +0.026]), so the simplest model wins. In MLB, current standing is the whole signal we can measure. **NHL: elastic net** (direct, on `y_doom_3yr`). It is the best NHL model; B1 is clearly worse (−0.088 [−0.178, −0.048]), and the more complex challengers are slightly but significantly worse: EBM −0.022 [−0.040, −0.003], horizon EN −0.012 [−0.027, −0.004]. Both chosen models are linear, so each team's score splits exactly into per-feature contributions. The EBM shapes are close to straight lines, which also favours the linear model. LightGBM finds no interaction worth having: depth 2 is no better than depth-1 stumps and is worse than the elastic net in both leagues. The NHL xG swap gains +0.021 Spearman, with an interval that just touches 0; it is not used because of the soft leakage. **Intervals:** the 80% conformal intervals cover 82.2% (MLB, B1) and 85.1% (NHL, elastic net), close to nominal and slightly conservative, but they are wide (about ±30 and ±26 doom points). Doom is a rough ranking, not a precise forecast. **3-year path (D78):** yes, from horizon models. NHL: three elastic nets (`y_pct_t1..t3`); each beats persistence (0.573/0.518/0.343 vs 0.513/0.438/0.301), and their implied doom is on average 0.7 points from the direct score, so in M3 the three projections are shifted by a common offset so that 100 − mean(path) equals the headline doom exactly. MLB: horizon elastic nets do not beat persistence, so the path uses three B1-style OLS fits on `win_pct_percentile` (one per horizon). Their mean reproduces B1's doom exactly when all three are fitted on the same rows, because OLS is linear in the target. **Note for M3:** with B1 chosen, an MLB team's driver breakdown has a single driver (current standing).
