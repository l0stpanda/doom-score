**NHL trained on 2008+ only** (no Corsi gap), compared on its test range 2016–2022: Elastic net: Spearman mean 0.634 (all seasons) vs 0.614 (2008+); L1 logistic: Spearman mean 0.595 (all seasons) vs 0.550 (2008+). Full table: `m1_nhl2008_comparison.csv`.

**Findings.** **MLB: the features do not beat current standing alone.** The elastic net's per-season Spearman is 0.404 vs 0.414 for B1 (Δ −0.010, interval −0.049 to +0.024), its MAE is 0.3 points worse, and the L1 logistic is slightly worse than B1 on AUC and Brier. The regularization agrees: most MLB coefficients are exactly 0 in most folds, and only run margin (−5.1 doom points per SD) is used every time. In MLB, doom is almost entirely "how good are you now", and run margin is a slightly better measure of that than the record. **NHL: the features help, clearly but modestly.** Elastic net Spearman 0.607 vs 0.519 (+0.088 [+0.038, +0.148]), MAE −1.2 points [−2.1, −0.3], AUC +0.033 [+0.007, +0.060]. The L1 logistic gains less, and its Brier interval includes 0. The NHL signal is stable: Corsi, margin, trailing record, roster age (older → more doom, +2.9), star concentration and draft capital never change sign across folds. Top-5 hit rates (about 2 of 5 in both leagues) do not improve significantly anywhere. Training NHL on 2008+ only is not better (Spearman 0.614 vs 0.634 on 2016–22), so the fill-plus-indicator default stays. **Caveats:** there are only 16 MLB and 10 NHL test seasons, and labels of adjacent seasons share up to two future seasons, so the season-bootstrap intervals are probably somewhat too narrow.
