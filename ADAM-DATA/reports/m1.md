**NHL trained on 2008+ only** (no Corsi gap), compared on its test range 2016–2022: Elastic net: Spearman mean 0.634 (all seasons) vs 0.614 (2008+); L1 logistic: Spearman mean 0.595 (all seasons) vs 0.550 (2008+). Full table: `m1_nhl2008_comparison.csv`.

**Findings.** Intervals below are from the moving-block bootstrap (D72). **MLB: the features do not beat current standing alone.** The elastic net's per-season Spearman is 0.404 vs 0.414 for B1 (Δ −0.010, interval −0.064 to +0.026), its MAE is 0.3 points worse, and the L1 logistic is slightly worse than B1 on AUC and Brier. The regularization agrees: most MLB coefficients are exactly 0 in most folds, and only run margin (−5.1 doom points per SD) is used every time. In MLB, doom is almost entirely "how good are you now", and run margin is a slightly better measure of that than the record. **NHL: the features help, clearly but modestly, and the gain survives the block bootstrap.** Elastic net Spearman 0.607 vs 0.519 (+0.088 [+0.048, +0.178]), MAE −1.2 points [−2.4, −1.0], AUC +0.033 [+0.007, +0.064]: all three intervals still exclude 0. The L1 logistic gains less; its Spearman interval excludes 0, its AUC and Brier intervals do not. The NHL signal is stable: Corsi, margin, trailing record, roster age (older → more doom, +2.9), star concentration and draft capital never change sign across folds. Top-5 hit rates (about 2 of 5 in both leagues) do not improve significantly anywhere. Training NHL on 2008+ only is not better (Spearman 0.614 vs 0.634 on 2016–22), so the fill-plus-indicator default stays. **Caveats:** there are only 16 MLB and 10 NHL test seasons. Blocks of 3 respect the label overlap, but with 10 NHL seasons there are only 8 distinct blocks and the first/last seasons are drawn less often, so the intervals are coarse and can be asymmetric around the point estimate (e.g. NHL MAE).
